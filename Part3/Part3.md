# Part 3 — Competitor Read

I selected **Quizlet** and **Khanmigo** because both have AI-assisted question/assessment generation, but they approach the problem differently. Quizlet is primarily a student study platform, while Khanmigo is positioned as an AI teaching assistant.

## 1. Quizlet

### What it genuinely does well

Quizlet has a broad workflow for turning existing study material into usable practice. Its AI tools can generate practice tests from notes and support multiple question types, difficulty settings, and time limits. It can also transform uploaded notes, slides, and PDFs into study guides, flashcards, and other study material.

This breadth is a genuine strength. Instead of treating question generation as an isolated feature, Quizlet connects generation to an established student study workflow. Users can also edit generated material, which gives them control over the final result.

### Where the marketing is ahead of the software

Quizlet describes its AI tools in terms such as helping students "learn faster" and "retain more," but the product material does not establish that every generated question is necessarily well-grounded, appropriately difficult, or pedagogically effective.

This is an important distinction for Scholera: **the ability to generate a large number of plausible questions is not the same as reliably generating good assessment questions**. A product can make question creation faster while still leaving teachers responsible for checking correctness, grounding, ambiguity, and quality.

## 2. Khanmigo

### What it genuinely does well

Khanmigo takes a more teacher-oriented approach. Its teacher tools can generate questions, discussion prompts, multiple-choice quizzes, answer keys, lesson material, and other instructional resources. Khan Academy also describes tools that can generate questions at different depths of knowledge and create additional questions from existing educational resources.

Its strongest feature is therefore **workflow integration**. Question generation is part of a broader teaching-assistance workflow rather than a standalone generator. This can reduce the time teachers spend creating instructional and assessment material.

Khan Academy also deserves credit for explicitly acknowledging the limitations of AI-generated educational content. Its teacher documentation notes that generated material may introduce inaccuracies, including in its question and quiz-generation tools.

### Where the marketing is ahead of the software

Khanmigo's positioning emphasizes saving teachers time and generating assessment material quickly. However, its own guidance makes clear that AI-generated content can contain inaccuracies and therefore requires educator review.

That means the difficult part of assessment generation is not solved simply by producing questions quickly. The remaining problem is determining whether each question is **correct, supported by the source material, unambiguous, and appropriate for the intended learning objective**.

## 3. What Scholera should do differently

Both competitors show that **question generation itself is becoming a commodity capability**. Quizlet can turn study material into practice tests, while Khanmigo can generate assessment questions as part of a teacher workflow.

I would therefore not position Scholera primarily around generating more questions or having a more powerful model. The stronger opportunity is **trustworthy, lecture-grounded assessment generation**.

Part 1 provides an initial reason to explore this direction: the Generate → Verify strategy achieved the strongest grounding and highest cognitive-demand score in the evaluated sample, although the sample was too small to establish production superiority. A Scholera workflow could therefore generate candidate questions, verify them against the professor's supplied material, remove unsupported or redundant questions, and let the professor review the remaining set.

The success of this approach should be measured by outcomes such as **grounded-question rate, instructor acceptance rate, rejection rate, question diversity, and time saved per accepted question**. The competitive advantage would then be measurable: not simply how many questions Scholera can generate, but **how many trustworthy questions a professor can actually use**.
